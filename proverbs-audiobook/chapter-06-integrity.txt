Chapter 6: Honest Scales—Integrity in Business

Key Proverbs Verses

Proverbs 11, verse 1: "A false balance is an abomination to the Lord, but a just weight is his delight."

Proverbs 10, verse 2: "Treasures gained by wickedness do not profit."

Proverbs 16, verse 8: "Better is a little with righteousness than great revenues with injustice."

Expanded Discussion

"False balance" was literal cheating in ancient markets. Today: inflated expense reports, hidden income, deceptive marketing, passing off inferior products. The principle isn't moralistic. It's practical.

"Does not profit" means ill-gotten gains cost more than they're worth: legal troubles, lost reputation, sleepless nights, destroyed relationships.

Modern Application

Real cost of dishonesty: Legal exposure and regulatory penalties. Reputation destruction, permanent in the digital age. Psychological burden of deception. Lost opportunities from those who won't work with you.

"Better is a little with righteousness": sustainable small success beats catastrophic big failure.

Key Takeaways: Integrity is profit, not just morality. Markets reward value creation, not extraction. Shortcuts usually lead to longer roads. Reputation compounds faster than money.

Action Steps: First, audit one business practice for ethical alignment. Second, identify where pressure cuts corners and resist. Third, build reputation for fair dealing as market differentiation. Fourth, walk away from deals requiring deception.
