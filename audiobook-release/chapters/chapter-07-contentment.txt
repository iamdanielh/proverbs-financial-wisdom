Chapter 7: Contentment and Moderation—The Middle Path

Key Proverbs Verses

Proverbs 15, verse 16: "Better is a little with the fear of the Lord than great treasure and trouble with it."

Proverbs 23, verses 4 and 5: "Do not toil to acquire wealth. When your eyes light on it, it is gone."

Proverbs 30, verse 8: "Remove far from me falsehood. Give me neither poverty nor riches."

Expanded Discussion

Pursuing wealth at all costs often "takes wings". Suddenly lost to market crashes, health failures, or family breakdowns. Contentment is assets that never depreciate.

"He adds no sorrow": wealth obtained righteously brings peace. Wealth obtained anxiously brings misery regardless of amount.

Modern Application

Trap recognition: "Just one more year at this job" never ends. "When I reach X dollars, I'll be happy" but X moves. Sacrificing health and family for dollars is a bad exchange.

The middle path: Agur's prayer. Neither poverty nor riches. Enough room to give, enough constraint to need God.

Key Takeaways: Discontent destroys wealth already possessed. Wealth from anxiety carries built-in sorrow. "Enough" is defined by needs, not comparisons. Long-term sustainability beats short-term maximization.

Action Steps: First, define "enough" numerically and stop when reached. Second, audit life areas: what are you sacrificing for money? Third, practice gratitude daily for present provision. Fourth, calculate replacement cost of relationships and protect them.
